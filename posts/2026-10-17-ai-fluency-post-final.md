# LinkedIn Post — Final Draft

Last week, I was struggling with my AI-assisted workflow. Things that used to go smoothly weren't working the way I expected. I kept wondering what was going wrong.

Instead of pushing through, I stopped and asked myself — am I actually using AI the right way?

That question led me to "AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations" by Anthropic on LinkedIn Learning. And I'm glad I found it when I did.

The course introduced me to the 4D Framework — four skills that make up AI Fluency:

Delegation — Deciding what I should do myself vs. what I should hand to AI
  - Problem Awareness: Understanding my goals before involving AI
  - Platform Awareness: Knowing what AI can and can't do well
  - Task Delegation: Distributing work to play to each other's strengths

Description — Communicating clearly so AI has the right context to help me
  - Product Description: Defining what I want — output, format, audience
  - Process Description: Guiding how AI should approach the task
  - Performance Description: Setting the tone and style of collaboration
  - Effective Prompting: Giving context, showing examples, specifying constraints, breaking tasks into steps, and asking AI to think before responding

Discernment — Evaluating what AI gives me instead of just accepting it
  - Product Discernment: Is the output accurate and relevant?
  - Process Discernment: Did AI approach it the right way?
  - Performance Discernment: Was the interaction style effective?

Diligence — Owning the final output, because it's my responsibility regardless of how I got there
  - Creation Diligence: Choosing the right AI system for the task
  - Transparency Diligence: Being honest about AI's role in my work
  - Deployment Diligence: Verifying and owning what I share

What I realized is that I was skipping most of these. I wasn't giving AI enough context about my problem. I was accepting its suggestions without really questioning all of them. And I was delegating the most parts of thinking, not just the tasks.

Now that I understand this framework, I feel more comfortable steering my own AI workflow. The tools will keep changing — new models, new restrictions, new capabilities. But these foundational skills stay relevant no matter what.

I'm starting my path toward the Claude Certified AI Fundamentals (CCAF) certification, and this was the right first step.

More to come as I learn.

# LinkedIn Article Draft — Claude 101

**Title:** The Last Time I Felt This Kind of Learning Momentum Was 15 Years Ago in a Video Game

---

The last time I felt this kind of momentum while learning something new was about 15 years ago, clearing blacklists in NFS Most Wanted.

If you've played it, you know the feeling. Every blacklist you cleared didn't just give you a checkmark — it unlocked a new part of Rockport city. New roads, new shortcuts, new connections between places you'd already been. You weren't just progressing. You were discovering how the map actually fit together.

That's exactly how this learning path feels right now.

## The Starting Point

Last week, I completed "AI Fluency: Framework and Foundations" by Anthropic — my first step toward the Claude Certified AI Fundamentals (CCAF) certification. That course introduced the 4D Framework for AI Fluency:

- **Delegation** — What work should I do vs. what should AI do?
- **Description** — How do I communicate clearly with AI?
- **Discernment** — Is what AI produced actually good?
- **Diligence** — Am I using AI responsibly?

These four competencies are how you collaborate with AI in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical, and safe. It was solid theory — but I didn't yet see how deep it went.

## The Map Started Filling In

Today, I completed "Claude 101 by Anthropic" — 18 topics covering everything Claude can do, from basic prompting to Projects, Artifacts, Skills, Connectors, Research mode, and all the surfaces where Claude lives (web, desktop, terminal, Slack, browser, Microsoft 365, design tools).

It's a lot of ground. But here's what surprised me:

**The course didn't introduce new theory. Every single feature mapped back to the same 4D Framework.**

## How Everything Connects

Struggling with generic AI responses? That's a **Description** gap — you didn't give enough context about your situation.

Blindly trusting AI output without questioning it? That's a **Discernment** gap — you need to evaluate, not just accept.

Choosing the wrong AI tool or mode for the job? That's a **Delegation** gap — Platform Awareness matters.

Connecting AI to your data without thinking about permissions and safety? That's a **Diligence** gap.

But it goes deeper than troubleshooting. The Claude 101 features themselves are built on these competencies:

**Description shows up at every level of persistence:**
- Prompts — you describe what you need right now
- Memory and Styles — persistent description at the user level
- Projects — persistent description at the work-stream level
- Skills — codified description at the methodology level

Each one reduces the amount of re-explaining you do. Same principle, different scale.

**Delegation evolves as Claude's reach expands:**
- Chat mode — you delegate conversation and drafting
- Cowork mode — you delegate complex, multi-step research
- Connectors — Claude reaches directly into your tools
- Research mode — you delegate an entire investigation

More capability means more delegation decisions. And every increase in what you delegate raises the bar on Discernment (is the output good?) and Diligence (is this responsible?).

## The Real Insight

The 4D Framework isn't just one course's model. It's the operating system underneath every AI tool and feature.

AI Foundations taught me the theory. Claude 101 showed me it's not theory at all — it's the practical architecture of how AI collaboration actually works, no matter which tool you're using.

And like Rockport city in NFS Most Wanted, every new topic I complete doesn't just add a pin on the map — it reveals roads connecting to places I've already been. The learning compounds. The map gets richer.

## What's Next

Two courses down on the CCAF certification path. The map is expanding. More territory to unlock.

I'm starting to build a visual mental model of how all these concepts connect — and I have plans for where that's going. More on that soon.

The tools will keep changing. The framework stays.
